# Deep Research Mode

Use this mode only for broad, high-effort, or near-systematic questions.

## Scope Before Search

Define the decision or review question, material and application boundaries, dates and languages, eligible study and cell types, outcomes, exclusions, and stopping rule.

Choose a mode:

- **Deep-lite:** focused decision support using the strongest readily available evidence.
- **Deep:** multiple source families, backward and forward citation checks, quality assessment, and a structured evidence matrix.
- **Near-systematic:** reproducible queries, screening log, explicit inclusion criteria, deduplication, quality or bias assessment, and documented gaps. Do not call it a systematic review unless the actual protocol supports that label.

## Staged Retrieval

1. Discovery: find reviews, canonical terms, and candidate primary studies.
2. Expansion: search aliases, mechanisms, references, and citing studies.
3. Verification: inspect original full text for every decisive claim.
4. Contradiction search: seek null results, alternative mechanisms, and boundary conditions.
5. Quality assessment: evaluate method validity, scope match, independence, selective reporting, and conflicts.
6. Extraction check: verify decisive extracted values against the source; use a second pass or second reviewer when the stakes justify it.
7. Saturation check: stop when new searches add no material evidence or the declared resource limit is reached.

Maintain a compact log containing source, exact query, search date, filters, result count, screened count, inclusion/exclusion reason, and deduplication method. Note language, database, full-text-access, and publication-bias limitations.

## Synthesis

Organize around the user's decision rather than paper-by-paper summaries. Separate established consensus, condition-dependent findings, contradictions, transferable analogies, untested hypotheses, and missing decisive experiments.

Finish with a bounded conclusion, confidence and its basis, and the single next search or experiment most likely to change the decision.
