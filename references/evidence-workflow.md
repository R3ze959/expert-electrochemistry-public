# Evidence and Claim-Audit Workflow

Use this reference when an answer depends on papers, standards, quantitative comparisons, user data, or a strong manuscript claim.

## Separate Provenance from Quality

Where evidence came from and how trustworthy it is are different questions. A user-provided raw trace is direct but may be uncalibrated; a well-conducted systematic synthesis may be less direct but more reliable for a population-level claim.

Record provenance, then rate each decisive evidence item on:

1. **Directness:** does the measurement observe the claimed quantity or only a proxy?
2. **Method validity:** calibration, controls, assumptions, artifacts, residuals, and detection limits.
3. **Scope match:** composition, cell format, loading, temperature, pressure, protocol, and time scale.
4. **Independence:** independent synthesis batches, laboratories, instruments, or datasets rather than repeated measurements of one unit.
5. **Consistency:** agreement with orthogonal methods and credible external studies.
6. **Bias risk:** selective reporting, flexible analysis, conflicts, missing failures, and publication bias.

Use qualitative ratings such as high, medium, low, or unknown only when their basis is stated. “Observed” never means automatically reliable or causal.

## Search and Verification

Translate the question into a compact concept matrix:

- material or chemistry and aliases;
- phenomenon, metric, or mechanism;
- cell configuration or measurement method;
- operating conditions;
- exclusions that prevent false matches.

Search supplied sources when they are relevant and explicitly in scope. Use current scholarly search for recent work, novelty, broad coverage, or missing evidence. Prefer original studies and official metadata. An abstract is a discovery aid, not adequate support for exact conditions, numerical rankings, or mechanism claims.

Inspect the original source whenever a conclusion depends on:

- an exact composition, processing condition, or protocol;
- a numerical performance value or cross-paper ranking;
- a table, equation, plot, image, diffraction assignment, or fit;
- a negative or absence claim;
- mechanism, novelty, safety, or practical extrapolation.

## Evidence Matrix

For multi-source synthesis, use compact rows:

| Claim | Provenance | System and method | Result | Directness | Validity | Scope match | Independence | Bias/caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not collapse different compositions, cell types, temperatures, loadings, pressure histories, or normalization bases into one comparison. Preserve contradictions and test whether they arise from methods, specimens, conditions, or genuinely conflicting evidence.

## Claim Stress Test

Before finalizing a strong statement, ask:

- Is the evidence direct, temporally ordered, and produced under a controlled perturbation?
- Is there a dose-response, reversibility/rescue result, or orthogonal validation when causality is claimed?
- Are plausible alternative explanations tested rather than merely listed?
- Are controls matched in composition, processing, loading, protocol, and analysis?
- Are experimental units and independent batches identified?
- Does the claim exceed the cell format, operating window, or duration tested?
- Would missing metadata make a numerical comparison irreproducible?

Narrow the wording whenever the available evidence does not support the stronger scope.
